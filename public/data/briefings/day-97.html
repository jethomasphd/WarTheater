Situation Summary

The defining facts of June 3 were a sharp intensification of the “ceasefire-under-fire” pattern that has become the war’s structural baseline. An Iranian drone-and-missile strike on Kuwait International Airport killed one Indian national and wounded more than sixty, heavily damaging a terminal and diplomatic missions and briefly closing the airport before Kuwait Airways and Jazeera Airways resumed from another terminal; Kuwait expelled two Iranian diplomats. The strike came in retaliation for U.S. “self-defense” strikes on Qeshm Island over June 2–3 — a CENTCOM hit on an Iranian military ground-control station — and a June 3 U.S. Hellfire strike on an Iranian oil tanker near the Strait of Hormuz, whose engine room the IRGC said was damaged. Both sides insisted the truce still held even as they traded fire: Vice President Vance said the ceasefire “remains in place but the U.S. reserves the right to launch defensive strikes,” while Defense Secretary Hegseth offered the window’s defining phrase — “for the most part, ceasefire means fire is ceasing.”
