The Ford Comes Home: 326 Days, a Presidential Unit Citation, and a Structural Drawdown

The USS Gerald R. Ford Carrier Strike Group returned to Naval Station Norfolk Saturday May 16 after a deployment of three hundred and twenty-six days — the longest U.S. Navy carrier deployment of the post-Vietnam era and the second-longest in the modern aircraft-carrier record, behind only the USS Midway at three hundred and thirty-two days in 1973. The CSG surpassed the USS Abraham Lincoln’s 295-day post-Vietnam mark on April 15. Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Daryl Caudle had used his May 14 House Armed Services Committee testimony to set the date publicly — “more than 320 days” — and the final count of three hundred and twenty-six came in five days earlier than the dashboard’s prior “end of this month” projection. SecDef Pete Hegseth attended the pierside ceremony and presented a Presidential Unit Citation. The destroyers USS Bainbridge (DDG-96) and USS Mahan (DDG-72) of DESRON-2 returned with Ford; the CVW-8 squadrons — VFA-87 Golden Warriors, VAW-124 Bear Aces, HSM-70, VAQ-142 Gray Wolves — had returned to their home air stations between May 11 and May 13. Per Navy Times, Ford will be reclassified from “deployed” to “post-deployment maintenance” on the USNI Fleet Tracker update of May 18.

Diplomatic Decoupling: Iran’s Five Conditions Stall the Tehran Track; Lebanon Extends 45 Days

The two diplomatic tracks of this war have now visibly decoupled. The Iran track was published into a stall on May 14 when Iran’s Fars news agency disclosed Tehran’s five non-negotiable conditions for any further direct talks with Washington: U.S. payment of war reparations; U.S. recognition of Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz; lift the blockade; lift sanctions; return seized vessels. The reparations pillar and the Hormuz-sovereignty pillar are not negotiating positions — they are deal-breakers, and both Iranian officials and Western observers understand them as such. The same day, Iran’s judiciary spokesperson asserted Tehran’s legal right to seize U.S.-connected tankers and Senior Vice President Mohammadreza Aref declared that the strait “has always been our property.” Foreign Minister Araghchi at the BRICS New Delhi summit accused the UAE of “direct involvement in aggression against my country.” Taken together, the May 14 disclosures convert Iran’s public posture from “continued back-channel via Pakistan” into “published collapse of any near-term direct negotiation pathway with Washington.” The White House response, per established pattern, will be non-acceptance.

The Lebanon track moved in the opposite direction. A separate security track convenes at the Pentagon on May 29; Round 4 political talks are scheduled for June 2-3.
